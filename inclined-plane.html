Box on an inclined plane

The box stays at rest while the friction needed, mg sinθ, is no more than the maximum static friction, μs·mg cosθ. That means it slides when tanθ > μs.

At this angle

Every angle (using your values)

“Min friction needed” is the static friction that must act to hold the box. “Min μs” is the smallest static coefficient that would keep it from sliding.

Theory

Resolving the weight

On a slope at angle θ, the weight mg is split into a part pressing into the surface and a part pulling along it.

N = mg cosθ pull along slope = mg sinθ

Static friction

While the box is at rest, friction adjusts to balance the pull, up to a limit.

f_s ≤ μs N f_needed = mg sinθ

When does it slide?

The box starts to slide when the needed friction exceeds the maximum static friction. The mass cancels out, so the result depends only on the angle and μs.

mg sinθ > μs mg cosθ ⇒ tanθ > μs (critical angle θc = tan⁻¹ μs)

Kinetic friction and acceleration

Once moving, friction has the constant value μk N (usually smaller than the static limit). Newton's second law along the slope gives:

a = g (sinθ − μk cosθ)

Motion down the slope

Starting from rest, s = ½at², v = at, so a slope of length L takes t = √(2L/a) and the box arrives with v = √(2aL).

Theory

Newton's law of universal gravitation

Every two masses attract each other along the line joining their centres. The force grows with both masses and falls with the square of the distance.

F = G m₁ m₂ / r² (G = 6.6743 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻²)

Newton's third law

The pull of the Earth on the ball and the pull of the ball on the Earth are equal in size and opposite in direction. The ball moves visibly because its mass is tiny, while the Earth's acceleration is negligible.

Gravitational field strength

Dividing the force by the small mass gives the acceleration it causes, g = F/m.

g = G M / r² (≈ 9.8 m/s² at Earth's surface)

Superposition

When more than one body pulls on the ball, the forces add as vectors. Here the Earth pulls down and the person pulls sideways and slightly down or up, depending on height. The person's pull is millions of times smaller than the Earth's.

Falling ball

From rest, a ball of constant acceleration g falls a height h in t = √(2h/g). Because g grows slightly closer to Earth, the program recalculates the acceleration at each small time step.

How it is calculated

Geometry: Earth centre → ball = R + h. Earth centre → person's centre of gravity = R + 1. Ball → person = √(l² + (h − 1)²). Each pair attracts with equal and opposite forces (Newton's third law). During the drop the program adds the pulls of Earth and the person on the ball and integrates the motion step by step (velocity Verlet), so gravity grows slightly as the ball falls.
